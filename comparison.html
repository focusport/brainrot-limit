When to use what

Honest advice.

You don't have to pick only one. Many people use two.

Digital Wellbeing

Good for broad limits.

Free, built in, and requires no extra permission. It is a sensible first step for app timers, Bedtime mode and notification control.

Classic blockers

Good for hard app bans.

If you want an entire app unavailable during certain hours, a full-app blocker does exactly that.

Brainrot

Good when the feed is the problem.

If you need Instagram for messages or YouTube for learning but can't stop the Reels and Shorts, per-video limits are the missing piece.

Why per-video beats per-minute

Time-based limits measure how long you were in an app. They can't tell reading a message from watching forty Reels. When the limit fires, it fires on everything, and you override it for a legitimate reason. Once overridden, the feed is right there.

Counting videos targets the behaviour itself, which is also easier to see: "I watched 38 Reels" is harder to rationalise than "I was on Instagram for 41 minutes".

Free reel limiter vs paid app blockers

Many paid blockers charge a subscription and work by locking a whole app or a time window. Brainrot is free, needs no account, and limits only the Reels, Shorts and Spotlight feed, so messages and long videos keep working. A paid app can still be the better fit if you need iPhone support, cross-device sync or a different kind of lock. Apps such as BrainPal count reels too, but list blocking at the limit as a Pro feature (about ₹99 a month, or about ₹83 a month on the yearly plan, at the time of writing). Check each app's current price and features before you decide.
