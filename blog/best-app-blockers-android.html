Best App Blockers for Android in 2026: How to Choose

"Best app blocker" depends on what you are actually trying to block. A tool that is perfect for locking a game can be useless against the Reels tab. Here are the main types, what each does well, and who each is for.

By Brainrot8 min readUpdated Oct 11, 2026

On this page

  1. The four types of Android blockers
  2. How to choose
  3. What to check before installing any blocker
  4. Using more than one

The four types of Android blockers

1. Built-in tools (Digital Wellbeing)

Free, preinstalled on most Android phones, and require no extra permissions. You get app timers, Focus mode and Bedtime mode. They are a solid first step. Their limit is that they work at the level of whole apps and total minutes.

2. Full-app blockers

These block selected apps entirely, often on a schedule or with a PIN. They're ideal for apps with no real utility during certain hours, such as a game during exams. They struggle with apps that mix a function you need with a feed you don't.

3. Friction apps

These add a pause, such as a breathing exercise or a question, before an app opens. They suit people whose problem is the automatic reach for the phone. They don't limit what happens once you're inside.

4. Feed-level limiters

These work inside an app. Brainrot is one: it counts Reels, Shorts and Spotlight videos and closes the feed at your limit, while messages and long videos stay usable. It is designed for people who need the app but not the feed.

How to choose

If your problem is…Look for…
An app you don't need at all (a game, a shopping app)A full-app blocker or Digital Wellbeing timer
Reaching for the phone out of habitA friction app, or removing the app from the home screen
Reels or Shorts inside apps you also needA feed-level limiter
Not being able to focus on work or studyScheduled focus sessions with an essentials list
Late-night scrollingBedtime mode plus a hard daily video limit

What to check before installing any blocker

  • Permissions. Many blockers need Accessibility or Usage Access. That's normal, but make sure the app explains exactly what it does with it.
  • Data. Does it need an account? Does it upload your usage? On-device processing is the safest design.
  • Escape hatches. A blocker you can switch off in two taps won't hold at midnight. Look for options that add friction to disabling.
  • Battery use. A reputable app shouldn't be near the top of your battery list.
  • Essentials. Make sure calls and messages stay available.

Using more than one

These tools aren't mutually exclusive. A common combination is Digital Wellbeing for Bedtime mode and notification control, plus a feed-level limiter for Reels and Shorts. See how they stack up on the comparison page.

DisclosureWe make Brainrot, so we are naturally biased toward feed-level limits. The type of tool that suits you depends on your habits. If you don't use short-video feeds, a different tool may serve you better.
