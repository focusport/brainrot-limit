How to Use Digital Wellbeing on Android (and Where It Falls Short)

Digital Wellbeing is already on most Android phones, it's free, and it needs no extra permissions. If you haven't tried it, start there. Here's how to use it, and an honest look at what it can't do.

By Brainrot6 min readUpdated Oct 11, 2026

On this page

  1. Where to find it
  2. The dashboard
  3. App timers
  4. Focus mode
  5. Bedtime mode
  6. Where it falls short

Where to find it

Open Settings → Digital Wellbeing & parental controls. The exact name can differ a little between brands (Samsung, Xiaomi and others tweak it), but it's always in Settings.

The dashboard

The first screen shows today's screen time, a breakdown per app, notifications received and how many times you unlocked the phone. Look at a few days. You'll probably find one or two apps account for most of the time.

App timers

  1. On the dashboard, tap the hourglass icon next to an app.
  2. Set a daily limit, for example 30 minutes.
  3. When the time is up, the app is paused until midnight.

This is great for apps you don't really need: a game, a shopping app, a news app. It's less good for apps that do two jobs.

Focus mode

Pick the apps that distract you, and Focus mode pauses them on demand or on a schedule. Useful for work hours. You can usually take a short break from it, which is both handy and, on a bad day, a weakness.

Bedtime mode

Schedules Do Not Disturb and can turn the screen greyscale at night. It makes feeds less appealing, and it's one of the better built-in features.

Where it falls short

The one thing Digital Wellbeing can't do is look inside an app. It knows you spent 70 minutes on Instagram. It doesn't know whether that was messaging a friend or watching 140 Reels. So the timer either hits everything or nothing, and you end up tapping "ignore limit".

That gap is why we built Brainrot: Reel Limit. It's meant to sit alongside Digital Wellbeing, not replace it: use the built-in tool for app timers and Bedtime mode, and a per-video limit for the feeds. There's a straightforward comparison here.
