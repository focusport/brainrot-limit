Screen Time for Students: A Realistic Study-Week Plan

Telling a student to "just put the phone away" ignores that the phone is also the lecture, the group chat and the doubt-clearing channel. A plan that works has to keep the useful parts and cut the rest.

By Brainrot7 min readUpdated Oct 11, 2026

On this page

  1. What actually eats study time
  2. A plan for a normal study week
  3. Where Brainrot fits
  4. Breaks that don't wreck the session

What actually eats study time

It's rarely the big, planned distraction. It's the two-minute break that turns into forty, usually in a Reels or Shorts feed. Longer videos at least end. Feeds don't.

A plan for a normal study week

  1. Decide your study windows. For example 6 to 9pm on weekdays and a morning block on Sunday. Fixed times beat "whenever I feel like it".
  2. Keep the phone where you can't reach it. Another room if you can. In a bag across the room if you can't.
  3. Allow only what you need. Phone, messages, and the one or two apps your course uses. Everything else waits.
  4. Use a short-video cap, not a ban, in normal weeks. 15 to 25 a day lets you have a break without losing the evening.
  5. Go to zero for exam week. It's only for a few days, and the first two are the hardest.

Where Brainrot fits

You can schedule focus sessions for those study windows so they start by themselves, with your essential apps still available. And you can set the Reels and Shorts limit to 0 for exam week while lectures on YouTube still play. More on the students page.

Breaks that don't wreck the session

Work in blocks of 25 to 50 minutes, then take a five or ten minute break that doesn't involve a feed. Walk, water, stretch, look out of a window. It sounds too simple to matter, but a break that ends on its own is what you're after.

If it's not workingLook at the numbers before blaming yourself. If you hit the limit at 4pm every day, you may need a lower baseline, an earlier cutoff or just more sleep.
