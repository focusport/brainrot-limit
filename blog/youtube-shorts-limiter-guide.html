How to Limit YouTube Shorts and Keep Long Videos

YouTube is one of the best free learning tools ever made, and Shorts sit right next to it. A regular timer can't tell a three-hour lecture from three hundred Shorts. Here's how to limit just the Shorts.

By Brainrot5 min readUpdated Oct 11, 2026

On this page

  1. Why standard app timers don't work for YouTube
  2. What YouTube offers natively
  3. Limit Shorts with Brainrot
  4. Suggested limits

Why standard app timers don't work for YouTube

If you set a 30-minute timer on YouTube, it cuts you off in the middle of a lecture or tutorial, exactly when you're using it well. So you disable it, and then it protects nothing. The thing you want to limit isn't YouTube; it's Shorts.

What YouTube offers natively

  • A Shorts feed time limit has been rolled out in some regions, but it is a simple timer and availability varies.
  • You can hide a few recommendations with "Not interested", though Shorts keep coming.
  • YouTube's break and bedtime reminders nudge you but don't stop you.

Check your own app's settings, since YouTube changes these often.

Limit Shorts with Brainrot

  1. Install Brainrot: Reel Limit and enable it under Settings → Accessibility.
  2. Set a daily Shorts limit, or use Unified mode to share one budget with Instagram Reels and the rest.
  3. Use YouTube normally. Brainrot tells vertical Shorts apart from regular horizontal videos.
  • Long-form videos: lectures, podcasts, documentaries, music and live streams play with no counter.
  • Shorts: each one counts. At your limit the Shorts feed closes.
