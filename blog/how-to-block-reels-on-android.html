How to Block Reels on Android (Instagram, Facebook and YouTube Shorts)

People ask this a lot, usually after an evening they didn't mean to lose. The short answer: Android has no switch for it, and neither does Instagram. There are still a few ways to get there, and they're not all equally annoying.

By Brainrot6 min readUpdated Oct 11, 2026

On this page

  1. Why there's no off switch
  2. Option 1: Delete the app and use the browser
  3. Option 2: A timer on the whole app
  4. Option 3: Limit just the short videos
  5. Which one should you pick?

Why there's no off switch

Reels, Shorts and Facebook's version are the stickiest parts of those apps, so the apps put them everywhere: a tab, the home feed, search, even inside Stories. You can say "not interested" to a video, but that only changes what you see next. It doesn't remove the feature.

Option 1: Delete the app and use the browser

It works, in the sense that the mobile site is clunky enough to put you off. You lose notifications and some features, and plenty of people reinstall within a week because they miss the messages. Fine if you only use the app to scroll. Not great if you actually talk to people on it.

Option 2: A timer on the whole app

Android's Digital Wellbeing can pause an app after a set time each day. Handy for a game. For Instagram or YouTube it tends to backfire, because the timer ends your messages and tutorials too, so you dismiss it and carry on. I wrote more about that in how to use Digital Wellbeing.

Option 3: Limit just the short videos

This is what Brainrot: Reel Limit does. It counts each Reel or Short as you swipe, and when you hit your daily number it closes that feed. Messages, Stories, profiles and long videos aren't touched.

  1. Install the app from Google Play.
  2. Switch on its Accessibility permission (Settings → Accessibility). This is how it knows a Reel is open and when you swipe. Here's what it can and can't see.
  3. Pick a number. Zero is a full block. If you're not ready for that, start at roughly 25% below what you do now.
  4. Choose one shared limit across all apps, or a separate one for each.
Worth knowingInstagram and YouTube update their apps a lot. Once in a while a layout change breaks detection until we ship a fix. If counting stops, update both apps and tell us.

Which one should you pick?

If you…Try
Only use the app to scrollDelete it, use the browser
Need the app for chats or learningA per-video limit
Mostly lose time at nightA limit plus a bedtime rule, see stopping Reels at night
